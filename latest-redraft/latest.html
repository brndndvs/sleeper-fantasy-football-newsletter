Commissioner's Notes

Diddy Party WEEK 1

Week 1 is officially in the books, and we've already got a 213-point explosion, a Monday night heartbreak, a Sunday night sweat, and a couple of benches that will be causing some problems in the group chat.

First up, Jahmyr-oquai absolutely detonates for 213.06 over MarlBurrow Man. SOME SPREAD. Josh Allen, Gibbs, and D'Andre Swift combined for over 116 points, with DJ Moore and the Pittsburgh defense piling on another 46.5. 100.60-point margin. MarlBurrow Man still manages 112.46, which is normally a pretty damn good opening week. Unfortunately, when the other side hangs 213, all you can really do is shake your head and start preparing for Week 2. After last year's 0-5 start, that's about as strong of a statement as you could make.

Then we've got Tee Time and Purdy Good, and this one goes right down to the wire. Caleb Williams puts up 37.26, and Tee Time actually enters Monday night holding the lead. Then Travis Kelce and the Denver defense combine for just enough production to flip the matchup, and Purdy Good wins 114.66 to 109.96. Four-point-seven. That's brutal. You go to bed Monday thinking you've got it, wake up Tuesday, check Sleeper, and realize your entire Week 1 came down to a handful of points. Jonathan Taylor and Breece Hall were huge for Purdy Good, while Saquon Barkley's 8.50 ended up being a pretty important number on the other side.

Throwsa Parks handles Just Take the $50, 128.22 to 81.74. Bijan and McBride have strong weeks, but the real story is what happens on the other side. Zay Flowers puts up 31.50, while Jalen Coker explodes for 37.80 on the bench. That's a brutal combination to look back at when the final score is only 81.74. Throwsa Parks moves to 1-0, and Just Take the $50 gets its first chance of the season to stare at a lineup and wonder what could've been.

Dak MVP szn and Balls gave us the Sunday Night Football sweat. Both managers had multiple players in Cowboys-Giants, so this matchup was basically being settled in primetime while everyone else was already done for the night. Dak only puts up 14.40, which is a little awkward considering the team name, because Dak isn't even the MVP on his own fantasy team. Derrick Henry is the MVP, dropping 40.80 and doing enough damage to secure a 142.20 to 132.00 win. Balls gets big production from JSN, Jaxson Dart, and Isaiah Likely, but De'Von Achane's 8.60 leaves just enough points on the table for Dak MVP szn to survive.

DomOfTheMFYear gets smoked by Ne-Tuten-yahu, 104.06 to 83.40, and the biggest problem was the top and bottom of the lineup. Matthew Stafford manages just 4.10, Houston's defense goes negative, and Christian McCaffrey doesn't provide the kind of monster performance you'd hope for. On the other side, Kyle Monangai puts up 27.40 on the bench for Ne-Tuten-yahu. Fortunately, it doesn't matter because the game isn't close enough for that mistake to cost the win. But 27.40 on the bench is the kind of thing that somehow gets brought up three months from now.

And finally, the defending champion. Puka and rally opens the season with 179.56 over Za Island Animals. Lamar, Jeanty, Justin Jefferson, Montgomery, and Christian Watson all deliver, with Watson and Jeanty leading the way. The result is 179.56, the second-highest score in the league. Za Island Animals put up a very respectable 130.02, but still lost by nearly 50. That's not really getting beaten because your team was bad. That's just finding out the defending champion came to play.

AND THAT'S WEEK 1.

213.06 at the top. 81.74 at the bottom. A 100-point blowout, a 4.70-point Monday night heartbreak, two primetime games decided in front of the entire league, and enough bench points to fuel several regret-filled waiver-wire searches.

Six teams are 1-0. Six are 0-1.

Nobody has won the league. Nobody has lost the league.

But Week 2 is here, and now we actually have some evidence.

See you next week.
