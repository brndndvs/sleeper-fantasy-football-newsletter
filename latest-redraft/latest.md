## Commissioner's Notes

Week 1 Diddy Party News

The season is finally here, which means months of mock drafts, keeper arguments, questionable trades and completely unnecessary confidence are about to be put to the test.

With everyone sitting at 0-0, it is probably too early to panic. That being said, some teams already have reasons to feel pretty good, while others may want to start preparing their excuses now.

Team to Watch: MarlBurrow Man

Nacho comes into Week 1 with one of the more intimidating starting lineups in the league. Joe Burrow, Kyren Williams, Puka Nacua and CeeDee Lamb give him a ridiculous amount of weekly ceiling. The problem is that three of those four stars are involved in some of the marquee matchups of the week.

If this team hits, it could be a long Sunday for the rest of the league. If it doesn’t, we’re going to hear about how fantasy football is mostly luck.

Team I’m Worried About: Purdy Good

Kahuna’s roster looks good on paper, but Week 1 is not doing him any favors. Jonathan Taylor gets Baltimore, Nico Collins gets Buffalo, and Terry McLaurin gets Philadelphia.

Those are three players who were drafted to carry a lot of the weekly scoring burden, and all three have difficult opening matchups.

The good news is Breece Hall gets Tennessee, Brian Thomas gets Cleveland and Justin Herbert gets Arizona. So this isn’t a disaster.

But if this team starts slowly, don’t be surprised if Kahuna spends the next seven days insisting he “actually likes his roster better after seeing them play.”

Best Week 1 Matchup: Tee Time

Tee Time might quietly have one of the better opening weeks in the league.

Saquon gets Washington, Ladd McConkey gets Arizona and the Chargers defense gets the Cardinals. Even Caleb Williams gets Carolina in what should be a much friendlier matchup than some of the other quarterbacks in the league are facing.

The only real concern is how much production is being asked from Quinshon Judkins and Xavier Worthy. If those two give him even average numbers, this could be a very strong opening week.

Sleeper Team: Puka and Rally

Clay’s team is interesting because the roster has a lot of young players who could make the league look very different by November.

Lamar Jackson opens against Indianapolis, Omarion Hampton gets Arizona, and Mark Andrews gets the Colts as well. The biggest question is Ashton Jeanty against Miami, but there is enough upside here that I would not want to face this team if Lamar decides to remind everyone why he is Lamar.

This feels like one of those teams that could either look terrifying or extremely questionable by Sunday night. There may not be much middle ground.

Biggest Question Mark: Ne-Tuten-yahu

Ne-Tuten-yahu may have one of the highest ceilings in the league, but the roster also has more uncertainty than it initially appears. Amon-Ra St. Brown and A.J. Brown give the team a strong foundation at WR, while Rashee Rice provides another potential weekly difference-maker.

The bigger question is the running back room. Jadarian Price has plenty of upside, but relying on a player who still has to establish himself as a consistent fantasy contributor creates some volatility. If Price becomes a legitimate weekly starter, this roster looks considerably stronger. If he doesn’t, the team could find itself depending heavily on its receivers every week.

The other factor to watch is quarterback. Patrick Mahomes gives this team another major piece of upside, but the overall roster could look very different depending on how the RB situation develops.

Bottom line: The ceiling is high, but this team needs its younger and less established players to hit. If they do, Ne-Tuten-yahu could be a legitimate contender. If they don’t, the star power at WR may not be enough to carry the roster all season.

Matchup I’m Watching: Just Take the $50

GoSports has one of the most volatile lineups in the league. Jayden Daniels against Philadelphia is not an easy start, James Cook gets Houston, and Ja’Marr Chase gets Tampa Bay.

The upside is obvious. The problem is that this team has enough players in difficult matchups that Week 1 could go in either direction.

If Chase goes for 30 and Cook puts up a big number, nobody will care about the matchups. If they don’t, expect the phrase “it’s only Week 1” to make its debut approximately 14 minutes after the Sunday games begin.

One Team Nobody Should Be Sleeping On

Throwsa Parks might have the most balanced starting lineup in the league.

Jalen Hurts, Bijan Robinson, Drake London and Trey McBride give Ethan multiple high-end scoring options, and Garrett Wilson has a very manageable matchup against Tennessee. The concern is that Bijan and London both draw Pittsburgh, which could make Sunday afternoon less comfortable than the names on the roster suggest.

Still, this is one of the few teams where there aren’t many obvious holes. That usually tends to be a good thing.

Week 1 Prediction

It is way too early to make serious conclusions about the season.

Naturally, that will not stop anyone.

There are already several teams that look like legitimate contenders, a few that need their stars to carry them through difficult matchups, and at least two rosters that could have everyone feeling very differently by Monday night.

By this time next week, we’ll either have our first legitimate contenders or 11 people explaining why their team is actually better than their record.
